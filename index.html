HOW TO PLAY

Objective

Identify the country of origin where military equipment was designed and manufactured.

⚠️ Identify where it was BUILT, not who uses it!

Many countries operate equipment manufactured elsewhere. Your goal is to pinpoint the country that originally developed and produced the equipment, not the countries currently using it.

How to Play

1. View the Equipment

An image of military equipment will appear on your screen. Study it carefully - the visual design can often provide clues about its origin.

2. Make Your Guess

Click anywhere on the world map to drop a pin where you think the equipment was manufactured. You can also click on a specific country for more precision.

3. Confirm Your Selection

Once you've placed your pin, click "CONFIRM DROP" to submit your guess.

4. Bonus Round - Identify the Equipment

After guessing the location, you'll be challenged to identify the specific equipment from multiple choices. Correct identification earns bonus points!

Scoring

  • Location Accuracy: Up to 5,000 points based on how close your pin is to the actual manufacturing location
  • Equipment Identification: Additional 5,000 points for correctly naming the equipment
  • Total Possible: 10,000 points per round

The closer you click to the correct location, the more points you earn!

Game Modes

  • Start Game: Play 5 random rounds at your own pace
  • Daily Challenge: A 60-second speedrun — identify as many targets as possible before time runs out. The same target set is shared by all players each day
  • Practice Hub: Browse and study all available equipment to improve your knowledge

Daily Challenge — Speedrun Rules

⏱ 60-Second Clock

You have 60 seconds to correctly identify the origin of as many targets as possible. The game auto-advances after every guess — no result screen, no delays.

❌ Time Penalty

A wrong country costs you 3 seconds off the clock. Accuracy matters as much as speed.

🔥 Combo Multiplier

Chain 4 or more correct answers in a row to activate a ×1.5 combo multiplier on every subsequent correct guess — until you break the streak.

Tips

  • Many countries produce distinctive equipment with unique design philosophies
  • Pay attention to details like shape, size, and visible markings
  • Western equipment often differs in design from Eastern equipment
  • Some countries are known for specific types of military technology
  • In the Daily Challenge, prioritise correct guesses over speed — a 3-second penalty hurts more than a slow correct answer

Good luck, and may your geographical knowledge be sharp!

Anonymous account ID: When the app loads, Firebase Authentication signs you in anonymously and assigns a random account ID (a Firebase User ID). It is used so that the leaderboard's security rules only let you write, and update, your own score — nobody else can overwrite or post a score in your slot. If you later sign in, this account ID is linked to your account (see section 3.6).

When you click POST SCORE on the Daily Challenge results screen, the following data is transmitted to and stored by Firebase:

  • Your chosen callsign (free-text, up to 16 characters — letters, numbers, hyphens and underscores only)
  • Your score for that day's challenge
  • Your account ID (described above)
  • A random installation ID generated locally by your browser and stored in Local Storage. This is a random UUID — not a hardware identifier — used to tell apart players who choose the same callsign. Clearing site data resets it.

  • A server-side timestamp generated by Firebase at submission

All of the above is publicly readable. Leaderboard entries, including the account ID and installation ID stored with them, can be read by anyone using the app, because they are needed to rank players and to open each player's profile card (section 3.5). Neither ID reveals your name, email or sign-in account.

No other personal information is sent. We do not transmit your name, email, IP address, hardware identifiers, location or any tracking signals with your scores. The callsign is freely chosen by you and need not identify you personally — you may use any pseudonym.

Firebase is operated by Google LLC. As Firebase's infrastructure provider, Google may log connection metadata (such as IP addresses) at the network level when your browser communicates with Firestore, in accordance with the Firebase Privacy and Security Policy and the Google Privacy Policy.

Your name, email address and profile photo are never shown on a profile card. The card is identified only by your callsign. If you do not want a profile card, do not post scores to the leaderboard. To have your public profile summary or posted scores removed, see section 3.8.
